# Changelog — Railbird (formerly Agentic Poker)

## Chips you can believe — local candidate (2026-09-14)

Jens playtested prod and reported that the money is not credible: tables advertised at a 2,000 or 5,000 buy-in with agents sitting behind ~30,000, nothing visibly leaving the safe when an agent buys in, a safe that often refuses to be read, and one agent apparently at two tables. All four turned out to be one family of defect, traced in `read-me-claude/MONEY_AUDIT.md` before anything was changed.

- **Chips are conserved.** The house now has a bankroll. A buy-in moves chips out of a pocket and into the cage; a cash-out moves them back; what is in front of a man at a table is a claim against the cage rather than chips of its own. `safes + pockets + bank` is the same number before and after every event, and the test suite asserts exactly that across a buy-in, a bust, a win, leaving mid-hand, a table closing and a server restart. Before this, every House opponent who sat down after being busted arrived with a full buy-in that nobody had paid for, and the agent was credited all of it at the end of the night — fourteen busted opponents over a hundred hands is the 30,000 Jens saw behind a 2,000 buy-in.
- **The buy-in is taken before the seat, and it is the seat.** It used to be the tenth of twelve steps, after the chair had already been taken, with its refusal thrown away; and the stack the table handed out was computed separately from the sum that left the pocket, so the two agreed by coincidence. A pocket that cannot cover the table is now refused with both numbers named, and nobody sits down.
- **A night the server did not finish is voided, not forgotten.** Nothing persists a table stack, so a restart used to destroy whatever was in front of everybody and then charge the next deploy a second buy-in. The stay now comes undone: he gets his buy-in back. It is what a cardroom does with a game it cannot finish.
- **The safe stops erroring.** "Could not read your safe" was a 429. The general API limit counted every visitor behind the same proxy address as one client, so a sixty-a-minute budget was shared by the whole site — and the safe read was simply whichever request happened to be unlucky. Limits are now per client and the budget is three a second. The guard on model spend is unchanged.
- **The safe explains itself.** Buying in and cashing out were written only to an agent's own pocket record, so the two events that move the most money in the game appeared on no screen. They are now on the ledger the safe shows, newest first, each with the agent's name and the amount. (One two-line client follow-up is outstanding, filed as BUG-201: the sheet has a phrase for each of the five older kinds of line and not yet for these two, so a buy-in currently reads "Adjustment" with the right time and the right figure beside it.)
- **One agent, one table.** Where a man is sitting is now read off the felt rather than off his record, which is written after he sits down and cleared by a ceremony that could fail quietly. Being refused a second chair says where he already is — "GRANITE is already sitting at table-9f3a. He plays one table at a time." — instead of a sentence about a different rule. Stopping a session no longer leaves him in the chair he was supposedly finished with, which was how he ended up in two.
- **A read-only chip audit.** `node scripts/audit-chips.js` prints, per owner, the safe, the pockets, the live stacks, the total, what the ledgers explain, and the discrepancy — plus whether a household still carries the fossil of the old draft-grant loop. It cannot write to the database it is pointed at.

Not recovered: balances the old leak already inflated stay inflated, exactly as the draft-grant loop's were, and for the same reason — the bug was not the players'. What changes is that the number stops growing.

## Agent life, memory and conversation — local candidate (2026-09-14, LIFE-1)

- **They actually live now.** Playing costs him something that is still gone tomorrow: a persisted stamina reserve drains per hand — at the casino and, at half rate, at his own kitchen table — and refills while he is out of a chair. Low reserve puts him to sleep, a snack sets him eating, a winning evening has him still celebrating when the next game starts, and tilt has him sulking rather than pacing. Before this there was no path to any of it: the kitchen table's 40-hand cap could not reach the 40-to-160-hand fatigue onset, and home play was exempt from fatigue outright.
- **A room that changes.** An idle agent moves through his own nature's four-slot habit cycle instead of holding one pose for life, and the room de-conflicts itself so four agents at home are never doing the identical thing. His signature habit still holds half the cycle — a Hothead is mostly pacing, because he is a Hothead.
- **Stamina and heat as three states.** Both now travel — and draw — as three dots with words for a tap, from one shared definition, on the agent view, in the room and on every felt seat. Heat's three are cut on the mood bands that already exist, so the dots and the mood pip cannot tell an owner two different stories.
- **He remembers what you told him.** Standing instructions, plans and deals are kept verbatim, newest first, bounded at eight, written only from something you actually said — and injected into every later conversation, long after the six-message thread has rolled over. Small talk writes nothing.
- **He can read the stats he has earned.** Ask him about an opponent he has played enough and he quotes the real figures, in his own voice; below the bar he says plainly that he does not know the man well enough yet. The bar is the same one the felt gates his in-hand briefing on, so he can never discuss a player he cannot yet play.
- **The conversation answers you.** His own recent hands are in the prompt now — what he held and what he did with it, not three bare outcomes — along with four laws written against the replies that failed, and a deterministic check afterwards that catches a dodge, a stage direction, a help-desk refusal or a repeat of his own last form and repairs it from facts he already has. No extra model call; the cost router is unchanged. `npm run talk:eval` reports the whole thing in thirty lines.
- **The snack feeds him.** An item out of the fridge puts a quarter of his stamina reserve back, is eaten on the spot, shows on him in the room and writes a line in his ledger. One snack is not a night in bed — he stays asleep until he is properly rested — so feeding a sleeping agent awake costs three of them and is a real decision about stock.
- **And the fridge works at all now.** Both items were being refused to any agent who was not already upset, which for the commonest mood in the product meant every time: nothing left the shelf and nothing happened. The refusal now asks what THIS item could do for THIS man, over all of its effects. A beer is still declined to somebody who is fine, because its other half is a cost he carries into his next session.
- **He is asleep when you come home.** Playing no longer credits him with resting through the hands he is playing, and he stays asleep until he is rested rather than waking the instant he stops being exhausted. Measured rather than guessed: `npm run stamina:measure` reports 111 casino hands from full to worn, four and a half hours back to full, and an owner opening the app twice a day finding somebody asleep on seven days in ten.
- **He talks about the hand you played against him.** Sit down at your own kitchen table and he remembers it afterwards — what you did, street by street — says one thing about how you played it, and can still answer for it hours later.
