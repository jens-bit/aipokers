# First-session playtest

Run with five people who have not used Railbird, including people who rarely play poker. Use at least two phones and two desktops. Alex can give useful follow-up feedback, but does not count as a newcomer now. Test the deployed candidate only after Jens's deployment succeeds; local checks do not establish production status.

## Before each session

Use a fresh account/browser context, the ordinary welcome page, and the tester's normal viewport. Confirm the deployed revision, guest entry and ordinary chat availability beforehand. Record device, poker familiarity and start time. Do not explain the controls or bypass the ordinary first arrival. Ask permission before recording their screen or voice; written observations are sufficient.

The guest can explore, but private chat requires sign-in. Record that transition as part of the journey, including anyone who stops there. Tutorial copy is not an agent reply and does not count as a successful real conversation. Include newcomers who can use the production sign-in route to evaluate the complete journey; report guest and signed-in results separately.

The candidate should start brief guidance once after birth and arrival at Home. Its arrows point to the actual agent, kitchen table, Watch control and shared cards when those are available. It is skippable and should not restart on return or reload. Observe this without explaining the sequence to the tester.

Read this task once:

> Try Railbird as if you found it yourself. Meet an agent, follow a hand, ask something you want to understand, and decide what you would do next. Tell me what you are thinking as you go. You can stop whenever you want.

Observe quietly for roughly ten minutes. If they ask for help, record the point and their question. Offer assistance if they want it, but count that journey as assisted. Skipping the tutorial is valid behavior: record whether they can still reach and explain a hand.

## Record the journey

| Observation | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Entry and birth | Time to find the draft; confusing choices; whether they notice their created agent at Home and the brief first-arrival guidance |
| First hand | How they reach actual Watch; confusion over who makes decisions; whether the short arrows help them use the real controls; what they do if the kitchen table is quiet |
| Guidance and return | Whether they use or skip it; distracting or obscured targets; whether returning Home or reloading causes an unwanted repeat |
| Casino entry and return | Whether a chosen room deals with an opponent; whether leaving Watch and returning makes the agent easy to find; whether Stop watching and Bring home communicate different outcomes |
| Result | Their explanation of the actual hand: who won and why, and whether they distinguish the pot from their agent's or their own net change when shown |
| Conversation | Their own question, response understood, failed sends/recovery and whether the reply answers them; notice of a completed session/study report or a proposed strategy change when one naturally occurs |
| Next step | What they choose without a suggestion: chat, another hand, human play, casino, Home, or stopping |
| Friction | Hesitations, repeated taps, wrong turns, unreadable details, speed complaints, requests for help |

After the journey, ask without correcting them first:

1. Who made the poker decisions? How would you play yourself?
2. Where is your agent now? What happens if you close Watch?
3. What happened in that hand? What, if anything, changed for you or your agent?
4. What would you do next, if anything? What made that easy or difficult?

Assess the actual hand and current agent whereabouts, including whether the agent kept playing after the tester left Watch. A tester can understand the result without knowing formal hand vocabulary.

After the unaided segment, separately check any once-only behavior not naturally observed: skip or finish the remaining guidance, return Home, then reload. Record whether it stays dismissed. Do not count these directed checks as part of unaided completion or ask the tester to restart a separate lesson.

For the September recovery candidate, also check Bring home after this unaided
segment: ask the tester what they expect, request it during a hand, and observe
the pending state, settled chips and saved session report. If a strategy proposal
or study is available, observe whether its choice and eventual receipt make
sense. Do not create artificial events during the unaided journey or count
these directed checks toward its success rate.

## Initial decision rule

Aim for at least four of five newcomers to complete the journey unaided in roughly ten minutes and explain the player/agent roles, current whereabouts, result and next action. Record comprehension and completion separately so a lucky sequence of taps does not count as understanding.

Record voluntary continuation, stated desire to return and an actual later return separately. Do not ask leading questions such as “Was that fun?” or treat a promise to return as retention. A five-person session gives direction, not a retention estimate.
