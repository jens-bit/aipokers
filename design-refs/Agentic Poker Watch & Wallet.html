Design wave 33

Tension, theatre,
and whose money
it is.

The playtest verdict on the watch screen was “a simulation, not a game”, and it was right for three specific reasons: everything appeared at once at machine speed, the money on the line was a small number in a corner, and the decision line read like a solver. Part 1 fixes those three and nothing else — the felt, the LiveBar row and every law survive.

Part 2 accepts the real shape of the product: most hands happen while nobody watches, so the flagged hand becomes a 28-second replay using the same pacing beats. Part 3 builds the economy that was specified and never drawn — an owner wallet, and a pocket per agent. Poker staking, backer and horse: the pocket is the bet, so there is no betting menu.

What this wave adds
P1Two tabs instead of four; four server-driven pacing states; the tug-of-war bar; his line in thread voice, ≤12 words. Plus the haptic and sound contract.
P2The replay theatre — same felt, same rope, same held reveal, scrubbable, entered from the recap.
P3Wallet, pockets, three funding modes, the collect moment, and broke drawn without guilt.
P4The 1440 overflow, and header heights specified in pixels so the port has a number to hit.
Bound by, not reopening

Fish-tank law. Mood law. Every character law from design 32 — no purchase path, attributes never gate play, the ceiling is never a number on a bar, cost lines are his misjudgment, biography touches voice and mood only. And the density verdict: a panel is a picture, not a paragraph.

Part 1 · Watch v3 — mobile
390×844. Chrome above the felt is 136px (40 header + 56 mood band + 40 tabs), so the felt gets 330 and the rope always sits under the board rather than under the fold. The table-id line is gone; street and to-call live in the LiveBar row.
33aCALMthe default
Identical to the felt that shipped, plus the rope and minus the noise. The tug-of-war bar sits directly under the board: him on the left in teal, the villain on the right in red, the seam animating on every street. His number is on his end; the villain end gets a name and no figure, because the owner is watching his agent, not refereeing the table. Below, READ — the opponent model that has existed server-side all along, finally on a screen.
33bHEATINGpot past the threshold
The felt warms, the ticker goes from 23px to 30px in a gold pill, and one rigid haptic fires — once per hand, never repeated. Nothing is added to the screen; the same elements get louder. The rope thickens to 12px because this is the moment its number matters most. Below the fold the READ panel gives way to the prediction beat, live while he thinks.
33cALL-IN3–5s hold
The only place this product raises its voice. A red breathing glow, his line promoted to Playfair 17 in a box of its own, RIVER IN 3 counting down, and a three-to-five second hold before the runout. The hold exists only while a spectator is watching — unwatched, the hand resolves at machine speed, because a pause nobody sees is just a worse win rate. The prediction locks the instant he acts: you called it.
33dSHOWDOWNreveal, then the slide
Cards flip one at a time, the reveal is held for a beat, then the pot slides to the winner — the ticker lifts and fades as the result card lands. His hand is named in words (“Ace-high flush”), the villain's is stated plainly, and the figure is the only large number. In the panel below, the READ updates in front of you: GOES TO SHOWDOWN moves 41 → 44, its confidence bracket tightens, and a new read forms in his voice.
33eNo read yetnever a spinner
The state the old screen got wrong twice. There is no “waiting for first action” and no “ready for next deal”: before any evidence the bars are empty with ·· for values, and he says “Give me a few hands.” The rope sits dead centre rather than blank — hero equity shows from the deal, never a dash while he is to act. How fast those bars fill is what the READS attribute actually does, which makes this the first screen where an attribute is felt rather than read.
W2Haptics & soundcontract, not a mockup
Ten events, and the phone is on silent in a bar — so every beat lands on haptics alone and sound is a second layer with a mute toggle. Three rules carry the table: haptics fire on his events only, never two inside 120ms, and losing is quiet. A loss sound is the product telling the owner off, and there is no guilt anywhere in this design.
Part 2 · Replay theatre
The same components as Watch v3, driven by an authored timeline instead of a server: PaceFelt, TugBar, his line, the held reveal. 28 seconds, scrubbable, street-marked. Entered from the thread recap and the flagged rail.
34aThe theatremid all-in
Scrubbed to 16.5s: the all-in, before the river. The scrubber is street-marked (PRE · FLOP · TURN · ALL-IN · RIVER · END) so a hand can be read as a shape before it is played, and the beats are authored where the tension is — the runout gets the same 3–5s hold a live spectator gets. The panel below shows the read as it stood at that moment, not as it stands now: FOLDS TO HEAT 8 is why he thought this would work.
34bThe revealace-high
Held, then the pot slides. The bluff is shown in gold rather than teal — the money was won, but what is interesting is the cards. “Ace-high. No pair.” and the fold named underneath. His closing line is the only text under the felt, and the scrubber has stopped rather than looped: a replay ends.
34cEntry from the recapa poster, not a link
“One hand did most of it and you missed it.” The replay card is the only new furniture the thread gains: the final board, the rope at 100%, the flag, one line, the figure, and a play button. It is a poster rather than a link — the hand is legible before you tap. Twenty-eight seconds, and he says so.
Part 3 · Owner wallet and agent pockets
Spec v11 §7.1, drawn for the first time. Two balances: the owner wallet is the player's money and buys items and slots; each agent carries a pocket, the roll he plays with, and pocket size sets his stakes. That is the whole mechanic — the pocket IS the bet, so there is no wager control anywhere in the product. Items come from the wallet, never a pocket: a pocket that can buy things is a purchase path into the character system.
35aThe YOU screenwallet + four pockets
One balance, then where the rest of it currently is. Each agent is a row: pocket, stakes, tonight's P&L, a mode tag, a drain bar, and a single action — Collect when he is up, Fund when he is not. Value Bot is cut off and his row is deliberately unremarkable: dimmed avatar, $0, no P&L, and the same Fund button as everyone else. Snacks sit at the bottom with the line that keeps the laws intact: bought from the wallet, never from a pocket.
35bThe funding sheetthree modes, and off
Top-up, allowance, auto-refill — and cutting him off as a fourth option with equal billing. Each mode is one sentence about what happens when the money runs out. The note at the bottom is the only rule the owner needs: a $500 allowance seats him at $10/$20. Bigger pocket, bigger stakes. The cut-off copy does the emotional work: he keeps his attributes, his read book and his grudges, so stopping him costs nothing but time.
35cHe brings it homepocket → wallet
“Six hundred and forty in my pocket. Three hundred of it is yours.” The collect card is drawn as a transfer, not a jackpot: his pocket on the left falling back to its float, an arrow, your wallet on the right taking the difference. One soft note, one success haptic, no burst and no coin. And the alternative is offered in the same row — leave it in, and he plays bigger tomorrow.
35dBroke, in the threadno guilt
“I'm out. Your call.” Four words, and then he drops it. The dock replaces the LiveBar with the plain fact — pocket $0, cut off Tuesday, nothing pending — and a Fund button that does not blink. He never pleads and the copy never scolds. His second line is the one that matters: my read book keeps either way. Nothing is lost by stopping, which is what makes stopping a real choice.
35eBroke, on the floorPOCKET $0
At the bar with a drink he is not enjoying. Sulking posture, a drink, and a small mono POCKET $0 chip at his feet — the only piece of money iconography allowed in the room, and it is a fact rather than a prompt. Balanced v2.1 plays on at the lit felt above him. The floor never asks for money; it only shows who is in the game.

The same four rows, unchanged.
