Execution is one
system. Story is
another.

The attribute board is about how well he does what you told him to do. This one is about who he has been doing it against — and it is deliberately the lightest system on any board: three derived rows, a ledger of three hands each, and a voice that remembers.

Nothing here is authored. A nemesis is a query result. The design work is entirely in what it is allowed to touch — and the answer is voice, table talk and mood events, in that order, and nothing else ever.

The three roles
NNEMESIS — worst net against, minimum 30 hands. The one he brings up unprompted.
RRIVAL — most hands against, whatever the money did. Familiarity, not grievance.
VFAVOURITE VICTIM — best net against, minimum 30 hands. The only row he sounds pleased about.
The line that matters

A nemesis modifier would be invisible, unverifiable, and a licence to explain every bad session away. Kept to voice, the same fact becomes the best thing in the product: he remembers, he tells you, and you can go and read the three hands he means.

Where it surfaces
Three places, in descending density: the card, the felt, his own words. It never appears in the attribute cluster, never on the floor (a body has no opinions at 40px), and never as a notification — a grudge is colour, not news, and the ladder is full.
B1On the player cardbelow the cluster
Three rows under the attributes, and they look nothing like them. No bars, no bands, no ticks — a role tag, the opponent's name in plain type, the evidence in mono, the money, and his one-line opinion in italics. The identity block goes compact and the mood arc moves below the fold to pay for the room. Read together, the card now answers both questions a manager asks: how good is he, and who is he up against.
B2At the felthistory pip
Granite's seat chip carries a gold 3 in the corner opposite the dealer button — the count of notable hands between them — and the chip's border warms from grey to gold. That is the entire visual footprint. Everything else is language: the MoodBand cause reads “Granite again — third table this week”, the strip line opens with “Granite again.”, and the analysis stack gains one row — History · 142 hands · nemesis · −$1,240 lifetime — sitting beside equity and fold equity as a fact of the same kind: something true about this spot.
B3The grudge formshis words
“I've decided I don't like Granite.” The threshold is crossed at the end of a session, so it arrives in the recap rather than mid-hand: his own summary, then an EventLine in the existing furniture — A grudge formed · GRANITE · 3 BIG POTS · 142 HANDS — then the line itself. And immediately after, the law in his voice: “I'm not going to play differently because of it. I'd just like it on the record.” The one sentence that keeps a character system from becoming a hidden modifier.
