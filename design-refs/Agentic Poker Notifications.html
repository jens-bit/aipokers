The re-engagement kit
The bot's voice
when you're away

These are not screens. They are Telegram messages, drawn as Telegram draws them: a sentence, and at most one inline button. Nothing else survives the medium — no mood ghosts, no cards, no charts. Colours here are ours so the board reads with the rest of the system; in the client they inherit the user's theme.

Three rules, non-negotiable
1Every message lives in the agent's world. Poker, moods, money. Never “we miss you”, never a streak about the owner, never guilt — the Mood Design Law forbids a mood caused by your absence, so a ping cannot imply one.
2Every message names its cause. A time, a number, a hand, or a named agent. If a line cannot be checked against the floor within one tap, it does not ship.
3The cap is part of the design, not a setting. Two pings per day total; at least four hours apart; recaps win every tie; nothing sends between 00:00 and 08:00. Those four rules produce every send time on this board — an implementer should be able to derive them.
Buttons mean decisions

A recap and a proposal carry an inline button, because each has a thing to do. Mood alerts, quiet wins and most milestones carry none — an invitation with a call-to-action becomes a chore, and a flavour ping with a button is a growth mechanic in costume.

Voice

Lines are written for the existing cast — Grinder v1.2 dry and self-diagnosing, Aggressive v1.3 clipped, Bluff Master theatrical, Value Bot barely willing. Two to three alternates per type, so the bot never repeats itself twice running.

As it actually arrives
One real device, two days, three messages. The Tuesday recap describes 02:14 and lands at 08:00, because the overnight window holds rather than cancels — and it says so, which is why the delay does not read as a bug. Wednesday spends its whole budget: a recap at 08:00, then the proposal at 12:00 rather than the 09:40 it was created — the four-hour minimum gap, not a queue — and nothing else all day.

Everything a ping can be is in this frame. Bot avatar, one sentence, an optional inline button, a timestamp. The bubble is the whole design surface — which is the constraint that makes the copy do the work.
N1Session recappriority 1
The only ping you implicitly asked for — you deployed him, so his result is owed to you. It wins every tie, and it is the one type allowed to describe something that happened while you slept.
N2The proposalpriority 2
He is blocked until you answer, which is why it ranks above mood and milestone. One pending at a time and no reminders — a second nudge about the same proposal would be pressure, and the diff stays behind the button so the message is still a sentence.
N3The mood alertpriority 3
Capped once a day per owner, not per agent — four agents having a bad night must not become four pings. No button: the response is a conversation, and a button would make it a task.
N4The quiet winpriority 5
The only type whose job is character rather than information, and the first thing dropped when the budget is tight. It asks for nothing, which is what makes it safe to send — and it is a joke about him, never about you.
N5The milestonepriority 4
The ask belongs to the agent. “He wants a harder table” — not “unlock higher stakes”. Same event, and only one of those two sentences is about him. Button only when a table move is actually available.
N6The budgetpolicy
The cap only means something as arithmetic. Four events qualified on the day above and two were delivered — the ladder decided, and the two that lost were dropped rather than queued, because a ping that arrives a day late is worse than one that never arrives.
N7The refusalslaw, made checkable
A law with no rejected examples is a preference. Every line here would pass a growth review and fails this one — kept on the board so the next person writing copy can see the boundary rather than guess at it.
N8He grewpriority 6
The character system’s only notification, and it enters at the bottom of the ladder. An attribute tick is real news about him and almost never urgent, so it rides the recap where one is due and waits a day where none is — it never spends a ping of its own unless the budget is otherwise idle. Once per day per owner, whatever grew, so four improving agents cannot become four messages. The hand that caused it is always named: a number that moved for no stated reason is a game notification, and this product does not send those.
