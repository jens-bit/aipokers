The confident face drew its brows rising at the inner corner — which is the universal worried brow. An agent who had just taken three pots in a row looked like he was about to be sick, and nobody could say why.

MOOD-2 gives every state a heat 0–100, so the answer is intensity, not more states. Five states × three tiers = fifteen faces, and the silhouette never changes in any of them: only eyes, brows and the glow move.

What the wave contains
15Faces. Each tier covers a heat range — held 0–33, plain 34–66, full 67–100 — so the art takes an input the backend already sends.
6Event expressions, 2–6 seconds, drawn over the state and never stored: stunned, smug-flash, locked-in, bored, wary, pleased.
4Sizes, with a subtractive rule: asymmetry drops first, then lids, then brows. At 24px the eye shape carries the mood alone.
Where the raised brow belongs

Stunned, and only stunned. Wide round eyes with the brows high and inner-raised is the face of a man who has just been outdrawn — perfect for two seconds after a bad beat, ruinous as a permanent expression.

One rule broken on purpose

Tilted at full heat is the only place in the system where a mood overrides its own colour token: the eyes go red. Nothing else reads as red-eyed, and the alternative was a sixth state.

The fifteen
Read down for the state, across for the temperature. The geometry lives in mood-atoms.jsx because it is the atom's own art — this board only renders it, so a drift in the atom shows up here first.
42Heat as a continuumseven steps
The tiers are a drawing convenience, not a mechanic. Heat is continuous and the glow tracks it smoothly even where the eyes step — which is why the strip reads as one agent over a bad hour rather than three pictures. Sulking is the row to notice: intensity closes the face there, where every other row opens it.
43Six expressions2–6 seconds
None of them is stored. An expression that persists becomes a state, and there are five of those — so these are the only faces that react to a single hand. Two break a rule deliberately: bored looks down and wary looks sideways, the only two faces that break eye contact with the owner. Card dead, he stops watching you; a nemesis sits down, he stops watching everything else.
44Four sizeswhat drops, and in what order
The rule is subtractive and ordered, so nothing is redrawn per size. faceDetail(size) is derived inside the atom, which means no call site can ask for full detail at 24px or strip it at 46 — and every existing caller across nine boards got the confident-brow fix for free, because none of them passes heat or event and the default lands on the mid tier at the caller's own size.
The landing page, redrawn from the faces
THE MOODS was one face per state, which undersold the system the moment it had three. Each card now shows the arc — held, plain, full, left to right, the middle one ringed — under a headline that leads with it: "Five states, and every one of them has a temperature."
45THE MOODSthree faces per card
The sub-line does the work the old one couldn't: each one runs from held to full — the same face, wound tighter. And the promise that matters most is unchanged and still last: he tilts because of bad beats and shown bluffs, never because you were away.
Wave 43 · the hands
Two small detached hands, no arms, hovering where a wrist would be and moving independently. They are body language at table scale — what he is doing with the hand — while the face stays the emotional readout. The split earns its keep: a hand pushing a stack forward reads at 20px, where no face does. Same ink as the body one step lighter, three-fingered mitten, 22% of body width. Geometry sits in mood-atoms.jsx beside the face system.
48Eight poses, three sizesand four for opponents
An opponent gets four — rest, hold, toss, push — and that is a rule, not an omission. He does not peek, drum, clench or cover, because those are the poses that say what somebody is feeling, and an opponent's feelings are the READ panel's job rather than the felt's. Detail drops with size the same way the face's does: at seat scale the finger circles are sub-pixel, so the hand becomes a plain mitten and position and rotation carry the pose.
49Push · three bet bands2 / 4 / 7 chips
The only pose carrying a quantity, and it carries it as height rather than a number — so a bet's size is legible on the body before the figure reaches the hero row. Three bands and no more, because eleven chips and twelve chips are the same gesture to a human eye. And all-in is not the top of the scale: it gets clench, since it is a different kind of decision rather than a bigger bet.
50Push, toss, cover4 frames each
These three need a strip because their meaning lives in the movement, not the shape — a still frame of any of them is ambiguous, and the other five read at rest. Cover is the one to notice: it passes through clench on the way up, which is the involuntary half of it, and it is the only pose that outlasts its own animation. On a win it inverts — one hand rakes the pot in instead. Hands travel toward the face on a loss and toward the body on a win, and that is the whole grammar of the pair.
51Brow triggerstwitch · lift · knit
One cause each — a brow that fires for two different reasons is decoration. Twitch is asymmetric on purpose, because asymmetry is what makes it read as involuntary. Lift is the only friendly brow the face owns: none of the five resting states draws both brows raised and level, so it is reserved for the moment he peeks at a strong hand. And knit is the only one that persists, because its cause does — it holds until heat drops below 55, which means it has to compose with all five states rather than briefly replace one.
