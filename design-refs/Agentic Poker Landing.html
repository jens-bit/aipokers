The screenshots are the build

Every device frame renders a real shipped screen component — BirthDraftScreenM, FloorOneScreenM, ThreadRestingScreenM, FloorThreeScreenM, DeskFloorLiveScreenM — scaled down inside the product's own iOS bezel. Nothing is redrawn. A marketing screenshot that drifts from the build is a lie with a long tail, and redrawing one guarantees the drift.

What is not here
1No testimonials. There are no users yet, so there are no quotes. A fabricated one would be the first thing a poker audience checks.
2No counts. No “12,000 agents drafted”, no live ticker of strangers. The floor screenshot shows your four agents, which is a claim about the product rather than its popularity.
3No earnings language. Not “win”, not “profit”, not “cash out”. The stakes language inside the screens is the game's own fiction; the footer says plainly that the chips hold no cash value and cannot be exchanged.
The one headline decision

“Deal him in.” Final. Three words of table language that are also the product instruction — you are being asked to put him in the game, which is exactly what the button does. The sub carries the whole mechanic (built in a 30-second chat, real hands, moods, memory, opinions), and the CTA reads “Draft him” with “Free · plays in Telegram” as microcopy. One clearly-fictional agent quote sits above the footer — named as an agent, never a customer — the only endorsement this product can honestly print.

The page
One long page, six sections, in the order a stranger needs them: who he is → how he comes to exist → what makes him different → where he lives → why it is credible → the legal floor. The mobile artboard is the same page and the same copy, not a reduced one.
