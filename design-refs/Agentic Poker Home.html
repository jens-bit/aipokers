Wave 47 · Home, round 2

The flat,
seen from above.

Round 1 was a waiting room. Agents stood in a line pretending to be a room, an agent at the casino was a chair with a caption, and nothing on the screen moved unless you did something. Three mechanics fix that, and all three are motion rather than layout.

1 · The home game

Whenever two or more are home they play each other at the kitchen table for nothing — real hands with the board 26 characters, hands, chips, bubbles and folds, at seat scale. One agent alone plays the house on the TV in the corner. There is no pot pill, no P&L and no money line at the table; the only label is FOR NOTHING. This is the fish tank: it is always moving, and it costs the owner nothing to watch.

2 · Away is shown

An agent at the casino is a framed live window on the wall — his table in miniature and moving, his seat lit and pulsing, cards landing, the pot in the corner, a brass plate reading 25/50 · +$340 · 41 min. Tap the frame to walk in and watch. Four away agents is a wall of pictures, and an empty hook is what a slot you are not using looks like.

3 · Routines, and the door

Idle behaviour comes from nature, and state overrides it: a Hothead paces, a Rock reads the paper, a Shark shuffles, a Grinder counts chips — while worn sleeps on the couch and busted faces the wall. Sending him out and coming home are both walks through the door, drawn as strips, because an agent who teleports to the casino is a setting rather than a character.

Two rules the layout now obeys
1One coordinate space, with fixtures declared. FLAT names the wall, table, couch, TV and door footprints, and occupants are placed against them. An occupant's y is his feet, not a distance from an edge — round 1 spent three passes on collisions caused by that confusion.
2A bubble picks its side from where it stands. Near the right wall it flips left, so it never clips the frame and never reaches into a neighbour. The recap frame proves it: he is at the door on the right and his bubble opens inward.
The room
Plan view, 390×470: the wall of frames along the top, the kitchen table centre, the couch and TV left, the door right. Everything an agent does happens in this one space.
R1One agent aloneplaying the house
With nobody to play he takes the couch and plays the house on the TV — the set is on, the felt inside it glows, and he is dealt in. Three empty hooks on the wall say what the other slots are for without a word of onboarding.
R2Two home, two awaythe home game
The two at home are heads-up across the kitchen table: three community cards, chips scattered, one of them talking. Two frames on the wall are live tables. Nothing at the table carries a number — the label reads FOR NOTHING, which is the difference between the two halves of this screen.
R3All four awaythe wall is full
Four frames, arranged as pictures. The room lights go down, the table is set with a dealt pair nobody is holding, and one line says NOBODY AT THE TABLE. The old floor's dead state was "Everyone's resting"; this one shows you exactly where everybody is, and one frame is HOT.
R4A recap waitingbubble flips
He waits by the door with his bubble up, and because he is at the right wall the bubble opens inward. Behind him the room carries on: one reading the paper, one asleep on the couch. Nothing about him waiting stops anything else happening — that is the difference between a room and a queue.
R5A return+$2,740
He comes through the door and his frame on the wall is gone — he is not in two places, so the picture comes down as he arrives. The session line rides above him, delta then pocket, and lands once. A per-hand result never appears in this room.
Routines, verbs and the door
The three reference strips behind the room. Every routine is a prop object on the agent rather than a screen he appears in, so a Hothead paces wherever he is standing.
R6My four verbssnack · whisper · send · rest
Everything an owner can do at home, and the reaction on the character — the mood law applied to the room. Note what is absent: no verb costs anything, none has a cooldown, and none of them is a chore you are punished for skipping.
R7Sending him out1.8s · 3 frames
He stands, crosses the room, and goes through the door. The home game reseats behind him — two players become one, and one plays the TV. An agent who blinks out of existence on his way to the casino is a setting; one who walks is a character.
R8Coming home1.6s · 3 frames
The reverse, with the session line above him from the moment the door opens. He takes a seat at the table, where the game already is — so arriving home means joining something rather than landing in a list.
