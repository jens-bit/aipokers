Wave 56 · one flow

The flow, in the
order it happens.

Board 29 was nine waves of rows stacked on each other, and reading it meant knowing which row had been superseded. It is now one linear flow: open, draft, born, home, sent out, the casino, his table, home again, the home game, a want, the safe, the fridge, sitting down yourself, a bet, retirement, and the floor. The numbered frames are the flow — read those in order and you have the product. Everything else on the board is a state or a sheet of the step it sits beside: a lettered frame is the same step in another condition, and the sections below the flow (carry, the guest, the share card) are mechanics that hang off it. Every frame is the current design; there is no second version of anything to choose between.

No count is printed here on purpose. Three waves running, the number went stale within a day of being written — an inventory has to be re-counted every time the board grows, and a rule does not.

Waves 47–53 are in archive/board29-history.html. The decisions that survived from them are written under the frame that embodies them rather than as a separate law list — a rule you can see obeyed is worth more than a rule you have to hold in your head.

The flow, in one paragraph

You talk, he arrives,
you send him out.

The chain has one primary action per screen and it names the next one. The draft ends in Deal him in; birth ends with him walking through the door; the room offers Send him to play; the door is the casino; the casino ends in Deal him in again, this time with a buy-in beside it. Nothing in the chain is a dead end and nothing needs a back button to make sense.

The room is where he lives, not a menu of him. Between sessions he is doing something — at the kitchen table, at the fridge, asleep on the couch, facing the wall after a bust — and that is the fish tank: it moves whether or not you do.

What the flow leaves out on purpose

No bottom nav: YOU is the avatar, the casino is the door, and the composer is the only thing at the bottom of the screen. No money on the home table. No prices on the furniture — the seat price appears in the table sheet and nowhere else. No labels under anyone's feet.

The first five minutes, then the week
The numbered frames are the flow, top to bottom. A lettered frame is the same step in another condition; the sections after the flow are mechanics that hang off it. Notes beside each frame say what it decides.

F01Open · nobody yetempty, not blank
An empty room is still a room: floorboards, the safe, the frames, the TV showing the casino, the door. One chair and one thing to press. The line under the chair is an observation rather than an instruction, and the only action names what happens next.

F02The draft opensglass, not grey
The draft is the board-26 glass sheet risen over the room, not a grey chat on a blank screen. The room stays behind it, dimmed to almost nothing, because he is not in it yet. The sheet covers the lower band only: the top is where he forms, and watching him form while you talk about him is the point.
F02bHe formsone atom, five sets
Silhouette after prompt 1, the hood after 2, the eyes after 3, the collar after 4 — and his colour with the name pill, held back for Deal him in. Four stages put colour and name on the same beat and neither got room; the collar is the beat that says he has stopped being a shape. All five are MoodGhost with different hood and glow parameters, so he cannot drift from the creature the product renders. The collar is the only added geometry.

The fourth answer gives him a collar: he has finished being a shape, but he is still grey and still nameless. This is the beat that makes the fifth one land — hold his colour back and it arrives as an event rather than as the last of four gradual steps.
F03bHe has a namedeal him in
The last bubble asks his name, and answering it turns the composer into one gold button. Colour, collar and the name pill arrive together, and the name he offers is one a pill can hold: “Granite. F07The casinotwo boards
Through the door, and still no bottom bar: ← HOME is where the back button goes. The board is two panels, two verbs. LIVE NOW is pots still building — the amount is “so far”, the row glows red when it is hot, yours carries a teal edge, and the tap is WATCH. TONIGHT is finished hands ranked by money, and the tap is REPLAY: a pot that is over cannot be watched, and offering to would be a lie about what the tap does. Under them the three rooms are doorways, and YOUR TABLE is his actual game — the ring, whose turn it is, the pot, the board, his own pill.
F07bNobody of yours init says where he is
The one state a live miniature cannot show. Rather than draw a table he is not sitting at, the block says where he is and offers the single action that changes it. LIVE NOW drops to the two pots actually running, and the header counts none of yours.

F10The home gamefour creatures
Two or more at home and they play each other at the kitchen table for nothing — real hands, real folds, no money anywhere on the table and no label saying so. Four agents, four hoods, four eye colours, all rolled at birth and never changing: mood moves the face, never the colour, so you can tell them apart while they are all tilted.

F11A wantheat 82
One want per agent, a sentence in his voice and never a bar. The tone is in the words and the face — at heat 82 "let me back in there" is a dare, and it reads as one because of the tilted face and the clenched hands, not because the bubble got bigger. The three answers live in the toast above the thread: yes, later, no. Saying no costs nothing, which is the whole difference from a need.

F12The safeone number, three verbs
Opening a safe answers one question — how much is in it — and offers the three things you can do about it: give, take, rules. The old sheet was a per-agent grid with the balance somewhere among four pockets and four refill rules. Under the verbs, tonight in three lines, and every figure carries the sentence that caused it: no number floats. Pull the sheet up for the ledger.

The 2nd seat at −10,000 sits in the same list as two beers at −60, because they came out of the same safe.

F13The fridgestocked, not unlocked
A fixture on the kitchen wall holding what you bought from the safe: beer and snacks, quantities, nothing else. No tycoon mechanics — a fridge is never upgraded. An agent answering "get me a beer" walks to it and then plays with the bottle beside his stack.

Taking a chair does not zoom the room: the screen becomes the Watch felt, the same seats and the same chips, with the hero seat replaced by you. Your two cards face up, your stack, your name pill — and no ghost of your own, because you are not a creature. Back is top-left, the thread is the paper plane top-right, and one of your agents reads you the way he reads anyone. No walls, no fridge, no fixtures.

F15BETthe one panel
Three of the four verbs are a tap; BET is the one that needs a number, so it is the one that opens a panel. It rises from the bottom edge into the verbs' own place and the felt above does not move — same cards, same rope, same ring on your pill. The amounts are named in poker's words with the figure under each, because half is the decision and 240 is only its size. The free field is for the one owner in twenty who wants 137.

A retirement is a chair that is empty rather than a slot that is locked. The room does not shrink, the others do not comment, and the frame he was in on the wall goes dark. What is gone is gone; the room simply has more space in it.

F17The floora room, not a list
Tap the sign and you are on the floor, looking down at it: six live felts, tiny bodies in the seats, a gold dot for each pot, the bar along the bottom wall with four standing at it, the stairs and the board in the top corner. A felt carries its stake and nothing else — the only other words on the drawing are the two fixtures, THE BAR and THE BOARD, which are things in the room rather than labels on tables. The casino was three doorways and a board before this: accurate, and completely abstract. Yours is the felt with the teal rim; the hot one glows red. It is never a map of all 1,600 — it is the room you are standing in, and the rest of the building is up the stairs.

19 · Carry him
The room has objects in it, so you should be able to pick one up. A long press lifts him; dragging lights one target at a time; the drop starts the routine the place implies. What being carried does to him is three parameters — tilt, float speed, hand pose — and no new drawing.
C1The liftlong press
Everything about the lift says held: he grows, his shadow separates from the floor and blurs 26px below his feet, and he says something about it — being picked up happens to him, and he has opinions. Nothing is lit yet; he has come off the floor and that is all.
C2Draggingone target lit
Five drop targets — couch, table, fridge, TV, door — and only the nearest one lights. Two lit targets is a question the room cannot answer. Each carries the verb it performs (REST, DEAL HIM IN, A BEER, WATCH TAPE, SEND HIM OUT) rather than its own name, because you are not choosing a place, you are choosing what happens. He is worn here, so he dangles: 14° of tilt and a 4.2s float.
C2bOver the doorthe edge case
The door sits at x352 of 390, and it is the target the other drags never exercise. His bubble flips to his left here — not because this frame special-cases it, but because the carried body now uses the room's own sideFor and bubRoom: the side comes from measured clearance and the width from what is actually free. Hardcoded to the right, as it was, this frame would have put the bubble 87px outside the phone.
C3The dropthe room takes over
He lands, the target goes out, and the routine the place implies starts on its own — dropped on the couch he sleeps, without a confirmation or a toast. The place is the instruction.
C4He refusesmid-hand
In a hand he does not come off the floor at all: the lift is rejected rather than undone, so there is no drop to watch and nothing to put back. A dashed rim and one line — “I am in a hand.” The alternative is letting you carry him and then snapping him back, which teaches you that the gesture is a lie.
C5Four statesthree parameters
Rested, worn, hot, in a hand. Dangles is −14° and slow, squirms is +6° and 0.9s, and a hand refuses. All three are parameters on the same body — nothing here is a second drawing of him. The lines sit under each cell rather than beside each body: in 62px cells no clamp can make four bubbles fit, and on a reference strip the line is a caption rather than room geometry.
