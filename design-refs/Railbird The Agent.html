Wave 63 · part C

Tap him, get him.

“It’s too much, I have no idea what is going on.”
— the owner, 7 Sep playtest

What he tapped was a chat page: a tiny avatar in a header, COMPOSURE and READS “upgrade” cards wedged between the lines of a conversation, and a profile that printed his name twice.

The fix is not a cleaner chat. It is a different idea: when you tap him, you get HIM. The character fills the upper half at the size he deserves, animated the way he is in the room, and the conversation is what is left over — his line first, in his voice.

What that costs the old screen
GoneThe upgrade cards leave the conversation entirely. They are one line each under RECENT on the profile, newest first, and the label is still the tap target for the old explainer — the explainer was never the problem, its position in the middle of a sentence was.
GoneThe second copy of his name. The sheet title is the name; nothing under it repeats it.
NewFour glass actions in one row, at the felt’s whisper-row height. Deploy carries its own answer — the stakes and his pocket are on the button, so it is not a question you have to leave to answer.
The measurements
84444 status + 40 header + 264 stage + 66 actions + 344 conversation + 76 composer. The stage never scrolls; only the lower half does, which is the whole of C3 — and it scrolls without a scrollbar, because a 15px desktop gutter inside a 390px phone is both a lie and 15px of bubble width.
RulesGlass only, no flat gray. Bubbles ≤ 150px, two lines, tail from the side. Names ≤ 6 characters on pills, bars anchored left. Identity never changes with mood.
The three that are not the sheet

Absence, the TV, the ceremony.

Absence (C6). An agent who is out does not become a caption saying he is out. His chair stays at the kitchen table with his name tag on it, dimmed, and his frame on the wall is lit — which is where he actually is. The room reports it; nothing labels it.

The TV (C7). One TV, two states, and the state is read from the room rather than set by a flag: somebody out, it carries his table; nobody out, it is the tape room. HomeFlat already derives this from its own children, so no screen sets it.

The ceremony (C8). The winner throws both hands up and holds them; the loser’s drop to his sides. Those are raise and rest — poses the hand system already had, so the celebration invented nothing.

Why a bust is bigger than a win
WinAdds something to the felt: hands up, a line, and above 100 bb three bursts over the table.
BustTakes a piece of furniture off it. The seat goes dark and his name pill falls off the table. Nothing is added, and that is why it lands harder than any amount of confetti would.
