The floor is home
on every device.

Same product, same mechanics, same metaphor as mobile — every atom reused verbatim: ghosts, mood system, felts, name chips, pot tickers, thread anatomy, tokens.

Desktop's only advantage is room. Mobile must choose between the floor and a conversation; desktop shows both. So a ghost click does two things: the camera zooms into the room exactly as it does on the phone, and his GameTile lights in the rail — same mechanic, two echoes, rather than two dialects. The rail's standing job is the record: StandupCard over the GameTile stack, one tile per live game, all visible at once. His thread is one more click, from the tile's chat action.

Birth, desktop — the same flow, one thing added
Composed entirely from existing parts: DesktopShell, DeskTopBar, ThreadRosterRail, Panel, PComposer, DeskFloor, PStandupCard. What desktop adds is not a bigger ghost — it is a ghost that gets to be the subject. Both platforms draw it at 168px; on mobile that instance is a 13%-opacity watermark behind the conversation, because a 390px column has no room to give it. Here it sits at full opacity in its own panel with the four traits beneath it, and the band, the rail row and the panel are all driven by one phase value — so they cannot disagree about how defined the draft is. Three additions to shared components, all props on things that already existed: ThreadRosterRail takes drafting / born, PStandupCard takes log, and ChatComposer takes suggestLead.
