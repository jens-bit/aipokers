Design wave 34

Somewhere
to press.

Playtest, 17:43–17:47, prod. Four findings, and the first one is the whole wave: after tapping a suggestion chip the recruiter said “Let’s go” and the screen offered nothing to press. The chain from draft to birth to floor to watch existed as screens but not as a sequence.

So this wave changes flow and emphasis, not systems. Design 32 and 33 stay exactly as they are — no attribute, no pacing state, no wallet rule moves. What changes is which thing is loudest on each screen, and what happens when you press it.

The four findings
F1Nothing pulls you forward. Fixed with one primary action per screen, named after the next screen — and the composer giving up its place once the brief is usable.
F2The birth card led with six bars that mean nothing to a new owner. The card is now about him; the sheet is a fold, closed by default on a first agent.
F3The forming ghost floated over the chat, didn't fit, and was then covered by the card. He now has a place — the band well, then the sheet's header well.
F4The floor: a newborn drawn twice, a bar that read as a queue of name tags, and a dead room. Four new rules, fish-tank law intact.
The line this wave will not cross

No tutorial screen and no text wall. Attributes explain themselves the first time each one matters — a cost line in the thread, a tappable label, one sentence. If it can't be taught in a moment where it already mattered, it doesn't get taught.

Finding 1 · the chain
Four screens, four primary actions, and each one names the screen it leads to. Read the strip first — the screens after it are the same thing drawn at 390px.
36aDraft · one chip inthe bug screen, fixed
This is the exact screen the playtest got stuck on. Tapping “Aggressive bluffer” is a decision, so the strip fills immediately — 78 / 71 / 34 / 82, never dashes — the nature forms and stops being a guess, and the composer gives up its place to “Deal him in”. Talking is still there as a text link under the button, one tap from the full composer with the brief intact.
36bThe cardabout him
Name, nature, his first words, one line of what he's built for. That is the whole card. Six bars are behind “His sheet” — closed, and on a first agent it is never open by default. The footnote is the onboarding strategy in one line: you can read the numbers later, he explains them as they matter. The action is the same verb as the draft screen, because it is the same intent, now confirmed against a name.
36cHe walks inone body
The floor does the confirming, as it always has — but now he arrives rather than appearing. One ghost, mid-room, a hairline trail behind him and an empty felt ahead. The standup says “Hothead v1.0 is taking a seat” instead of a net figure, for exactly this one moment. Primary action: Watch him.
36dHis first handthe chain ends
Thirty seconds old, and the only exit is into conversation. Composed from Watch v3's parts untouched — same felt, same rope, same hero row. His line is the one that closes the chain: “Right. Let's find out what I am.” READ is empty and says so in his voice, which is also the first time an attribute is doing visible work.
Finding 2 · the sheet, behind curiosity
Two states of the same card, and the only onboarding this product gets.
37aThe fold, openeda tap
The numbers are a drawer, not a headline. Opened, the fold shows the six bars in canon order with one line under them — the gold band is how good he might get — and nothing else. Note what a newborn's sheet actually looks like: every current sits well under its band, which is the shape of an agent who has everything to prove. The label changes from IF YOU WANT THE NUMBERS to SIX ATTRIBUTES · THEY GROW.
37bFirst cost linetap FOCUS
This is how an owner learns what Focus is: the first time it costs him money. The cost line already existed from wave 32 — the change is that its label is tappable once, and the tap produces one sentence, his current value, and the band. Then the label goes back to being a label. No tutorial screen, no carousel, no text wall, and nothing to dismiss.
Finding 3 · the ghost gets a place
He floated, didn't fit, and got covered.
37cBefore birththe band well
Two places, and they were both already in the design. Before birth he lives in DraftBand's 42px well — the slot MoodBand uses for a mood ghost — at the size it was drawn for, filling as the phase rises. Behind the chat he is a watermark at 8%, clipped to the scroll area, so he can never cover a bubble or be covered by a sheet. After birth he moves to the card's own header well (36b), and the card rises from him.
Finding 4 · the fish tank, for legibility and pull
Four rules added to the fish-tank law rather than replacing it. One ghost per agent, always — an agent has exactly one body, and an arriving body IS his body. Names are earned: a name chip appears when he is seated or selected, never worn at the bar, where posture is the identity. A live felt is the loudest object: everything else drops to 42% under a scrim and the live felt gets a lit rim on top of it. A resting room still breathes: never "Everyone's resting" — the standup says what happened, and a ghost with news carries one pip.
38aResting, and alivefour bodies, no tags
“Four resting · Bluff Master grew tonight.” The bar is three bodies at three different bobs with drinks and postures — no name tags at all — and the one who can't play sits apart in the corner. Three pips carry the only news in the room: +2 GREW, WORN, POCKET $0. No news, no pip. The room stays at 92% because nothing is competing with it, and there is one thing to press.
38bOne live felt42% everywhere else
There is exactly one place to look. The lit felt keeps its diorama, its pot ticker and its name chip at full brightness with a lit rim; the room, the bar and the corner go to 42% under a scrim, keeping their postures and their pips but losing their names. This is the same room as 38a with one thing turned up rather than a different screen.
38cThe newborn, oncewas drawn twice
The bug: a big ghost near the rail AND a copy of him in the seated lineup. Now there is one body, at floor scale, crossing the room with a trail behind it and a name chip on — because he is the selected agent for this moment. Balanced v2.1 keeps playing at his felt. An arriving agent is not a special effect; he is the agent.
